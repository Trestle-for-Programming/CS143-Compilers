

## Policies and Procedures

| Question | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **What is the course policy on collaboration?** | What you submit for grading is expected to be your original work. See the collaboration policy for details on acceptable/unacceptable conduct. The Stanford Honor Code is taken seriously; violations go to the Office of Community Standards. |
| **Can/should I work with a partner on the programming projects?** | Yes, you may work with **one partner**. Requirements are the same regardless. Both partners receive the same grade. You are responsible for understanding all code, even parts you didn't write. Choose a partner you trust. To switch partners, notify staff before the next assignment. Partnerships must be declared before the first assignment; no need to re-notify if you keep the same partner. |
| **How do we submit assignments?** | **Programming Assignments:** Electronic submission on `rice.stanford.edu` UNIX systems, due by **11:59pm** on the syllabus date. Build/test there — grading happens in that environment. <br><br> **Written Assignments:** Electronic submission on **Gradescope**, due by **11:59pm** on the syllabus date. |
| **What about late assignments?** | See the separate late policy for details. |
| **How are assignments graded?** | Mostly **correctness** based on output. We `diff` your output against our solution. Test thoroughly using provided test files and your own. Little time is spent on style/design feedback — good coding practices are expected as habit. Submissions that don't compile or fail to execute receive **limited partial credit**. |
| **What about regrades?** | **Eligibility:** Single, simple mistake causing multiple test case failures. <br> **Deadline:** Within 1 week of returned assignment. <br> **Requirement:** Written explanation justifying regrade. <br> **Partial credit restored for:** Fixing small bugs that lost many points. <br> **Not eligible:** Implementing missing features (even if small code changes), fixing serious problems (changes in several places). |
